# tailor studio

## what it is

tailor studio is a desktop app that turns a folder of photos from a clothing
shoot into finished grailed listings. it groups the shoot into items, reads
each piece's attributes off the photos, drafts the listing, looks up what
comparable pieces actually sold for, and fills in grailed's sell form.

## why

i sell clothing on grailed, and every listing took me fifteen to twenty
minutes. none of that time was the interesting part: sorting a shoot into
piles by item, writing another description in the same shape as the last one,
digging through sold listings to work out what to ask, then typing all of it
into a form field by field. do that across a stack of pieces and the
afternoon is gone.

## how it works

i point it at a shoot. it groups the photos by item, so a shoot of several
pieces comes back as one item per piece. for each item it reads what it can
see in the photos — what the garment is, its colour, its condition — and
drafts a title and a description from that. it looks up comparable pieces
that have actually sold, so the price starts from real numbers rather than a
guess. then it fills in grailed's sell form.

and it stops there. i read every listing, fix what's wrong, and submit it
myself. that's deliberate: it's a form-filling assistant, not an
auto-poster. i don't want something posting under my name while i'm not
looking, and the last stretch — is this price right for this piece, is this
description honest — is the part that actually needs me.

## what came of it

a listing takes a few minutes now instead of fifteen to twenty. i use it
every time i shoot, and a few close friends and family use it too. it makes
no money and isn't meant to. it's a personal tool.

## how i built it

i built it with claude. i found the problem, scoped it, set the bar for what
counted as good enough, and steered the build; claude wrote most of the code.
the parts i spent my own attention on were the accuracy tests for the ai
steps, and a sweep across model configurations to find the cheapest setup
that still cleared the accuracy targets i'd set.
